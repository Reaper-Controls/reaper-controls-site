Features

  • Closed-loop, not on/offGrim reads coolant temperature and adjusts fan speed to hold it, instead of slamming the fans on at one temperature and off at another.
  • Two outputsRun dual fans on separate outputs, with room for setups that need more than one Golem.
  • Uses the sensor you already haveBuilt around the same coolant temp sensor Holley uses. On a Holley EFI car it's designed to share the existing sensor; everyone else gets one in the box.
  • A/C awareA dedicated input brings the fans up when the A/C is on.
  • Run-on after shutdownKeeps the fans going after you shut off to deal with heat soak. Ships at a sensible preset, adjustable in the app.
  • Built to survive the summerSealed against the heat cycles, vibration and moisture that kill cheaper controllers a couple of seasons in.

Pairs with

  • Golem for brushed fans up to 40A per output
  • PWM brushless fans, no Golem needed

About Grim

The aftermarket fan controller market isn't short on options. It's short on ones that hold up. Cheap sensors drift, connectors corrode, and a controller that worked fine on the bench fails quietly two seasons later under a hood full of heat cycles and vibration.

Grim is being built around sealing and long-term reliability first, with closed-loop control and app setup on top of that foundation rather than instead of it. The goal is a fan controller you install once and stop thinking about.

In English and Scandinavian folklore, the church grim is the guardian of a churchyard, said to be the spirit of the first creature buried there, most often a black dog, set to watch over the ground. People who glimpsed it took it for an omen of death. It was standing watch the whole time. Grim does the same job under your hood: it watches the temperature and acts before the heat ever reaches you.
