Features

  • Reads almost anythingHolley and ACES square waves, the weak LS PCM output, points, coil negative, even an alternator W terminal. The input squares up whatever arrives against a fixed 2V threshold.
  • Drives the gauges nobody else willCurrent-triggered tachs, the kind in early Mustangs and Shelbys, Datsun 240Zs, and Smiths gauges in MGs, Triumphs and Volvos, sense coil current through the gauge itself. Every adapter we know of says it can't drive them. Púca has an output built for exactly that.
  • A real coil-negative pulseOlder voltage-triggered tachs often want the inductive spike a real ignition coil makes. Púca generates one.
  • A clean 12V square wave tooModern gauges, ECUs, shift lights and data loggers get a regenerated, textbook square wave, at the same time as the analog gauge.
  • Fails safe by designIf the input ever gets stuck, the outputs stop rather than staying on. That's built into the circuit, not added by software.
  • Nothing to configureNo firmware and no app. Wire the output your gauge needs and leave the others capped.

Which output do I use?

  • TACH HVYour tach's trigger wire went to coil negative.
  • I-DRIVEIt went to coil positive, or the gauge has two heavy terminals.
  • SQ OUTModern gauge, ECU or shift light.

Púca passes pulses one for one. If your tach reads half or double, the gauge or controller needs setting for your engine's pulse count. On an LS into a V8 gauge, that's what Hob is for.

Works with

  • GM LS PCM
  • Holley Terminator X
  • Holley Sniper
  • ACES
  • Points
  • PerTronix Ignitor I & II

Not supported: transmitter-box tachs (early Sun, Stewart Warner, Radatron), raw VR sensors, and multi-spark ignitions read from coil negative.

About Púca

A modern engine swap leaves you with a modern controller and, very often, a dashboard full of gauges that no longer work. The tach is the usual casualty: the controller puts out a tidy logic-level square wave, and a gauge built in 1968 is waiting for something else entirely.

Púca sits between them. It reads nearly any tach signal a controller or ignition puts out, and produces whichever waveform the gauge on the other end expects: a high-voltage coil pulse, a current pulse, or a clean 12V square wave. Prototype boards are in hand; we'll post measurements on real gauges once they're tested.

In Irish folklore, the púca is a shapeshifter, appearing as a horse, a goat, a hare, whatever form suits the moment. Feared as a trickster, it was also said to help those who treated it well. That's the whole idea here: one device that takes the shape of whatever signal an engine puts out and whatever signal a tach expects.
