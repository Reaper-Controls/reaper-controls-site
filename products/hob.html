Hob

Schematic Complete

The strong answer to LS swap tach problems.

LS swaps break tach signals in predictable ways: the pulse count doesn't match what a V8 gauge expects, the signal is too weak to register, or it's noisy enough to make the needle jump around. The usual advice is a pull-up resistor — it moves the needle, but it leaves the underlying signal problems in place and puts the PCM's output pin directly on the harness with no protection in between.

A hob — short for hobgoblin — is a household spirit from British and Irish folklore that quietly tends to the hearth and keeps a home running right, so long as it's treated with respect. Get on its bad side and it turns mischievous. It's a fitting name for a device whose entire job is to take a signal that's misbehaving and quietly put it right.

Hob is built to be the real answer instead: an active signal conditioner that takes a weak or wrong RPM signal in and puts out a clean, correctly-scaled signal your tach, shift light, or other RPM-dependent device can actually trust.

Doubles the pulse count. LS controllers put out 2 pulses per revolution; a V8 tach expects 4. Hob doubles the signal so the tach reads correctly without retuning the PCM or reconfiguring the gauge.

Protects your PCM. Series resistance and a Schottky clamp guard the input, and the outputs have their own drivers. The PCM pin isn't electrically connected to either output, so a short on a dash wire hits Hob, not your PCM.

Ignores noise. A Schmitt-trigger input has a dead band between its on and off thresholds, so ignition noise doesn't turn into false pulses or dropouts.

Light on the PCM. The PCM sinks about 0.6 mA per pulse, compared with about 14 mA for the common 1k pull-up resistor.

Two independent outputs. Two separate 12V square-wave outputs, each with its own driver, so you can run a tach on one and a shift light or other RPM-based device on the other.

Signal light. An LED glows dim with power and brighter when pulses arrive, so you can confirm signal with a glance under the hood.

No software, no setup. Hob is built from fixed-function logic, with no microcontroller and nothing to program. It's a potted module, and we recommend mounting it close to the PCM.

The design is complete and headed to PCB layout. These are design specs; we'll post bench measurements once hardware is in hand.
