Features

  • Protects your PCMSeries resistance guards the input, and the PCM pin isn't connected to the output. A short on a dash wire hits Hob's output driver, not your PCM.
  • A strong signal on the long runHob switches at about 2V and puts out a full 12V square wave from its own driver. Mounted near the PCM, the weak part of the signal travels inches; the run to the dash carries a strong one.
  • Easy on the PCMHob asks about 1.2–1.5 mA of the PCM's tach pin per pulse. The common 1k pull-up resistor asks about 14 mA, from an output whose ratings you can't look up.
  • Survives wiring mistakesA reverse-polarity diode and surge suppressor protect the power input, and the output driver survives being shorted.
  • Nothing to programNo microcontroller and no software. Wire it in and it works.
  • Set your tach to 4-cylinderHob passes the LS controller's 2 pulses per revolution one for one. Most aftermarket tachs have a 4-cylinder setting; if yours doesn't, change the tach output in the PCM tune.

Hob or Púca?

  • Hob: built for LS swaps. Turns the LS PCM's weak tach output into a clean 12V square wave and protects the PCM.
  • Púca: the universal translator. Reads nearly any tach signal and drives nearly any tach, including vintage current-triggered gauges.

Both pass pulses one for one, so the tach or the tune sets the pulse count.

Works with

  • LS Gen III
  • LS Gen IV
  • 12V square-wave tachs
  • 12V RPM inputs

About Hob

LS swaps break tach signals in predictable ways: the signal is too weak to register, it's noisy enough to make the needle jump around, or it drops out under hard throttle. The usual fix is a pull-up resistor. It moves the needle, but it leaves the signal problems in place and puts the PCM's output pin straight onto the harness with nothing in between.

Hob is the real fix for the signal: an active conditioner that takes the weak LS output in and puts out a clean 12V square wave your tach can trust, with the PCM pin kept off the harness. Pulse count is set at the gauge or in the tune. Rev B pilot boards are ordered, with bench and in-car testing next; we'll post measurements once testing is done.

A hob, short for hobgoblin, is a household spirit from British folklore that quietly tends the hearth and keeps a home running right, so long as it's treated with respect. Get on its bad side and it turns mischievous. Fitting, for a device whose whole job is to take a misbehaving signal and quietly put it right.
